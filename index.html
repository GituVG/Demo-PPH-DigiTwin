A four-window digital twin for postpartum hemorrhage

Risk isn't a single number.
It's a record over time.

Postpartum hemorrhage (PPH) is a preventable maternal emergency, but electronic health records treat delivery as a boundary — antepartum labs, intrapartum events, and postpartum vitals are rarely linked into one evolving picture of risk. This demo shows what changes when they are.

Four clinical windows, one patient

Rather than assessing risk at a single moment — most often at hospital admission — this model treats pregnancy and the postpartum period as one continuous record, organized into four windows:

Antepartum
Hemoglobin, platelets, fibrinogen, placental findings, prior history
Intrapartum
Labor duration, mode of delivery, augmentation, blood loss at delivery
Immediate postpartum
Vitals, fundal tone, coagulation labs, lactate — the first 24 hours
Extended postpartum
Hemoglobin trends, retained tissue, delayed bleeding, biomarkers — up to 12 weeks

Why mitochondrial DNA

Cell-free mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) is released when placental or vascular tissue is under stress. Because it lacks the packaging of nuclear DNA, the immune system reads it as a danger signal — a mechanism well documented in trauma and sepsis, and actively being tested here as an early marker of the physiology that precedes hemorrhage.

Explore Explorer and Scenarios to see how this and other signals move a composite risk score, and which window they come from.

About this demo. The scoring model here is a simplified, illustrative weighting of established risk factors — built to demonstrate the four-window concept, not a statistically validated clinical instrument. It is intended for teaching and internal discussion, not patient care.
